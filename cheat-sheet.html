1. Welcome & Purpose

5–10 min

Set expectations, make Tim comfortable, explain the purpose.

Say: "Thank you so much for taking the time to meet today. The aim isn't to leave with a month's worth of content. Today is about building the foundations so creating content becomes much easier and more sustainable from here."

Walk them through today:

VisionSocial media goalsWho we reachBranding & visual identityTone of voiceContent pillarsWorkflowNext steps

2. Understanding Funding People

15 min

Fully understand the organisation before touching social media.

Tell me about Funding People in your own words.

If someone had never heard of it, how would you describe it?

What makes Funding People different?

What are you most proud of?

What impact happens behind the scenes that people don't usually see?

Listen out for: repeated words, values, mission, emotional stories, passion points. These shape the messaging later.

The gold: ask for one specific story, a win Tim is proud of, and a lesson that cost the organisation something. Specific stories become the best posts.

3. Goals & Priorities

10 min

Understand what success actually looks like.

Say: "Before we decide what content to create, I'd love to understand what success actually looks like for Funding People."

Imagine we're sitting here in twelve months and social media has been a huge success. What has happened?

If Tim mentions followers, ask: "What would having more followers allow Funding People to do?" Keep asking "why" until we reach the real goals:

Attracting fundersBuilding trustCreating partnershipsIncreasing visibilityStrengthening credibility
Also: agree how we will measure it. One or two simple signals (enquiries from funders, partnership conversations) beat chasing follower counts.

4. Audience Mapping

10–15 min

Understand exactly who we are talking to. Three groups:

Primary: potential funders   |   Secondary: community organisations, partners, local authorities   |   Future: volunteers, supporters, general public

What does each audience care about?

What would make them trust Funding People?

What questions are they asking?

What problems are they trying to solve?

5. Brand Identity & Visual Direction

15 min

A simple visual identity that makes future content easy and consistent.

Say: "Before we create content, I'd love to make sure we're aligned on how Funding People should look and feel online."

Colours: any already associated with Funding People? Any to avoid? Do the current ones still feel right? Note primary, secondary, accent.

Fonts / feel: should it feel friendly, professional, modern, community-led, approachable?

Imagery: real people, community events, volunteers, behind the scenes, workshops, illustrations, graphics?

If you had to describe Funding People's visual identity in three words, what would they be?

WarmTrustworthyHopefulApproachableCommunity-ledProfessionalModern
Also: ask Tim to show me two or three accounts (any platform) he admires, and what he likes about them. Real examples beat abstract words.

6. Brand Personality & Tone of Voice

10 min

How Funding People should sound online.

If Funding People was a person, how would they speak?

FriendlyProfessionalHopefulHonestEncouragingPassionateCalm

How do you want people to feel after reading one of your posts?

InspiredSupportedInformedConnectedHopeful

Finally: are there any words or phrases we'd like to avoid?

7. Content Pillars

15 min

Agree four or five themes that all future content is built around.

Say: "Rather than thinking about individual posts, I like organising content into themes so everything feels more consistent."

Candidate pillars for Funding People:

Community Stories: success stories, people, community impact

Funding Insights: advice, funding opportunities, educational content

Behind the Scenes: the team, meetings, day-to-day work

Impact: achievements, outcomes, testimonials

Tim's Perspective: reflections, community issues, thought leadership

8. Workflow & Content Process

10 min

A content process that feels realistic and sustainable.

Where do ideas currently live?

How often would it feel realistic to meet?

How quickly can content be reviewed?

Would approving content monthly feel easier than weekly?

What would make social media feel less overwhelming?

Also: confirm the approval route here. Does sign-off go through Zoe, Tim, or both, and what turnaround can they commit to? Nail this and nothing stalls later.

9. Wrap Up & Next Steps

5 min

Summarise everything agreed, then lock next steps.

Say: "Today we've agreed..." then recap: who we're speaking to, key goals, brand personality, visual direction, content pillars, and the workflow.

Then agree: my next steps, Tim's next steps, timescales, and the date of our next catch up.

Within the hour: the move that looks premium

The recording is not the win. The follow-up is. Within an hour of the call, send Tim and Zoe a short tidy summary: what we covered, what we agreed, what happens next.

First-timers forget to follow up. Doing it fast and clean makes me look like the most organised person they've worked with. The AI notes make this a five-minute job.
