1Getting in and your account

Log in at meg.foreshiloh.com with your email and password.

  • My account (top right) is where you and your clients change passwords. New passwords need at least 8 characters.
  • When you create a login for a client, the portal gives you a temporary password to pass on. Encourage them to change it in My account on first login.
  • Log out is next to My account. Sessions last two weeks, so on your own devices you will rarely need to log in again.

2How a post travels: the approval journey

Every post moves through the same six stops. This is the heart of the whole system.

Idea → Draft → Needs approval → Client approved → Approved → Published

  • Idea and Draft are private. Only you can see them. This is your workshop, experiment freely.
  • Push to client is the moment a post becomes visible to the client and they are emailed to review it.
  • The client can do exactly two things: approve, or request changes (they must say what to change).
  • Client approved is not the end: you give the final sign-off, so nothing is official until you say so. Two locks on every door.
  • Mark it Published once it is live on the platform, so your records match reality.
Why two-stage approval matters: the client can never accidentally publish, skip a step, or approve something you have not finished. The portal physically refuses any shortcut.

3Your clients page and adding logins

All clients (manager view) is your front door: every client, with a count of posts awaiting approval.

  1. Add a new client with just their name. Their whole workspace is created instantly.
  2. Add a client login (their name and email) for each person who should see the workspace. They only ever see their own company's content, never anyone else's.
  3. Pass on the temporary password the portal shows you. They can change it in My account.

4Onboarding a new client automatically

A ten-minute questionnaire that builds a new client's strategy while you make a cup of tea.

  1. On the clients page, click Onboarding link next to the client.
  2. Copy the link and send it to your client. It works for 30 days and needs no login.
  3. They answer friendly questions about their business, their voice, their audience, and a story they are proud of.
  4. The portal then fills in their brand and voice guide, builds their audience map, and proposes content pillars and starter post ideas for you to review. You get an email when they finish.
It never overwrites you: anything you have already filled in stays exactly as you wrote it. The questionnaire only fills empty spaces.

5The content calendar

One board per client showing every post at every stage.

Creating a post

  • Fill in the title, the copy, the channel, the pillar, and an optional publish date.
  • Attach an image or video if you have it, the client will see the real thing when they approve.
  • Choose the starting status. Idea or Draft keeps it private to you.

Your private drafts strip

The pink panel shows Idea and Draft posts only you can see. Each has a Push to client button, that is the moment it goes for approval and the client gets their email.

The main board

Everything the client can see, column by column. Posts waiting for your final sign-off have an approve button right on the card.

6Writing with AI, in the client's voice

The portal writes first drafts the way your client actually sounds, because it reads their brand guide first.

  • Draft with AI on the calendar: type what the post is about, pick channel and pillar, and a full draft appears in about fifteen seconds, in your private drafts.
  • Draft with AI on any idea in the idea bank: same thing, one click, using the idea and its notes as the brief.
  • The AI reads the client's voice guide, pillars, audience, and their best-performing posts before writing a word. The better you fill in the brand guide, the better it writes. Garbage in, garbage out; gold in, gold out.
The golden rule: nothing AI-written is ever visible to a client until you have polished it and pressed Push. You are always the editor in chief.

7How your client approves, without logging in

The part your clients will love you for.

  • When you push a post, every client login gets an email containing the post, any image or video, and a personal magic link.
  • They tap the link and land on a branded page, no password, and either approve in one click or write what needs changing.
  • You get an email the moment they act, whichever way they did it.
  • Links are personal, expire after 14 days, and become harmless once the post moves on. Clicking an old link just says "already handled."
  • Clients who prefer the portal can still log in and approve there, both routes work.

8The idea bank

Where raw ideas live so you never face a blank page.

  • Add an idea with a pillar, a format, and a priority. It is private by default.
  • Share with client makes an idea visible to them, useful for "what do you think of these?" conversations. Unshare pulls it back.
  • Draft with AI turns any idea into a full draft in the client's voice, one click.

9Pillars, audience, and the brand guide: the portal's brain

These three pages look like admin. They are actually what makes everything intelligent.

  • Content pillars are the four or five themes every post hangs from. The AI tags meeting ideas with them and writes within them.
  • The audience map records who the content must reach, in priority order. The AI writes with these people in mind.
  • The brand and voice guide is the client's personality on paper: how they sound, what readers should feel, words to avoid, colours (type a hex code and a colour swatch appears). Every AI draft is built on this.
Best use: spend twenty minutes making these three pages properly rich for each client, ideally straight after their onboarding questionnaire or discovery meeting. Every AI feature gets sharper for it, forever.

10Messages

One running conversation per client, with email doing the reminding.

  • Each client workspace has a message thread between you and their team.
  • When you send a message, they get an email with a preview and a link; when they reply, you get one. Nobody has to remember to check the portal.
  • Replying to the notification email goes to the real person, not to a machine.

11Meeting notes: record, review, send, and mine for content

Your calls become notes, actions, and next month's content, automatically, but always through your hands.

Recording

  • Record straight from the browser during or after a call, or upload an audio file.
  • The portal transcribes it and drafts a summary plus suggested actions. The audio file is deleted once transcribed, only the words are kept.

Review, edit, send

  • Every note has a Review and edit panel. Tidy the AI's draft first, it is a draft, not gospel.
  • Email these notes sends your edited summary to whoever you tick (client, yourself, Zoe or anyone else). Nothing sends until you press the button, and sent notes are stamped so you can see what went where.

Content from this meeting

  • The portal reads the transcript and proposes three to six posts based only on what was actually said. Each has an Add to calendar as draft button.
  • Anything it could not verify appears as a [placeholder] for you to fill, it never invents facts.
  • Older recordings have a Generate content ideas button to run the same magic retrospectively.

Archive

  • Archive moves old notes out of the way; the Archived tab (with its counter) is where they live. Unarchive brings them back; Delete is forever.

12Analytics and the monthly client report

The proof of your value, gathered in one place and turned into a report clients actually read.

Getting numbers in

  • LinkedIn: open the company page analytics, press Export, and drag the downloaded file into the portal. That file is called a CSV, it is just a spreadsheet, nothing scary.
  • Or add a row by hand for any month and channel.

AI insights

  • Generate insights reads the numbers, the posts, and the pillars together and tells you what is working, what is not, and what to do next. Only you see this.

The monthly report

  1. Press Generate this month's report on the analytics page.
  2. The portal gathers the month's numbers and shipped posts and writes a warm, honest summary addressed to the client. It never invents figures; when data is thin it says it is early days.
  3. You edit the narrative until it sounds like you.
  4. Tick the recipients and send. It arrives as a designed, branded email, and the report is stamped with who received it and when.
Best use: send it the same week every month. It is the single most powerful retainer-keeping habit you can have, and it now takes minutes.

13The morning digests

The portal does the chasing so you never have to nag.

  • Every morning at 8, clients with posts waiting get one polite email listing them, each with its one-click approval link.
  • You get your own summary of posts awaiting your sign-off or carrying change requests, across all clients.
  • When nothing is waiting, nobody gets an email. Silence means all clear.

14The golden weekly workflow

How all the pieces click together into one calm routine.

The loop that runs a client beautifully

  1. Meet the client, record the call. Notes and actions draft themselves; you tidy and email them the same day.
  2. Open Content from this meeting and add the best proposals to the calendar as drafts.
  3. Top up with Draft with AI from the idea bank for anything else the week needs.
  4. Polish every draft in your own words, attach the visuals, then Push to client.
  5. Approvals happen from their inbox; the morning digest chases anything that stalls.
  6. Give your final sign-off, publish on the platform, and mark posts Published.
  7. Once a month: import the analytics, generate insights for yourself, and send the client their report.

16Quick answers

  • A client forgot their password: for now, ask Daniel to reset it; a self-serve reset is on the roadmap. They can change it themselves any time via My account once logged in.
  • An approval link says it is not valid: it has expired (14 days) or the post has moved on. Push again or use the portal.
  • The AI wrote something odd: edit it, that is the design. Better brand guides produce better drafts.
  • Does anything send automatically? Only notifications and the morning digest. Content, notes, and reports never leave without you pressing the button.
  • Something broken or a feature you wish existed: email or WhatsApp Daniel. This portal grows around how you actually work.
